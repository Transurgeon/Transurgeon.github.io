### Summary
A lot of exciting things happened this year.

I first completed the courses portion of my Master's degree.
I learned a lot of things and gave a review of each class I took [here]({% 2025-05-20-GradClasses %}).

In the meantime, I applied to a scholarship to do a visit with Prof. Stephen Boyd and received the great news in the middle of the semester.
I talk about some of my experience during the visit [here]({% 2025-12-12-GreatWork %}).

I also took a bit of a long break during the summer and attended the ICCOPT conference with Steven Diamond, Philipp Schiele and others.
I was very fortunate to stay at Chris Yeh's apartment in Caltech for almost a week.
We took the 45 minutes commute to downtown LA everyday.
We had a lot of fun throughout the sessions and also got a chance to try out different restaurants.

Then I also went on a Banff trip with Andre Kaba, Xinyi Guo and Xinjun Ye, friends I met in Montreal.
It was absolutely amazing, the views of the mountains and lakes there are quite literally stunning and the hikes were also very fun.

I finished my visit at Stanford through Atlanta, where there was the annual INFORMS conference.
I met many more solver developers such as Ben Champion (HiGHS), TC Zhang (COPT), and Chris Maes (CuOPT).
There I was staying at my friend April's GATech friends apartment (thanks for hosting me, Vivek, Thiago and Emil).
It was another really nice experience and I also got to catch up with other fellow conference attendees from last year's ISMP.

Finally, I came back home in November and took a bit of time off.
I kept working on DNLP and resumed things for my master's project with Antoine.
More importantly though, I spent a lot of quality time with my family, and restarted playing hockey; it has been an absolute joy.

### Reflections
This year, I finally managed to complete a yearly goal: do well and learn new things in my master's classes.
Although to be fair, I did set an easier one (and more subjective as well) to have better chances at it.

However, this year I did manage to complete an much more difficult goal from two years ago: contribute in a paper.

My second goal was to get a clearer idea of what I will write a thesis about. 
Unfortunately, this did not happen at all, and in fact, many interesting things have surfaced which might make this goal even more difficult for next year.
I hope to write a blogpost about this whole master's experience at some point.

Overall, it was a good year with lots of ups and certainly a few downs too.
More importantly though, I have enjoyed many aspects of this journey and continued spending time with awesome people.

### Goals for the year
To start the year off, Daniel and I have been working on a differentiation engine in C.
This has been an exciting idea on our minds after we released a draft for DNLP.
My first goal for the year will be to integrate the engine into the DNLP repository, with eventual goals of merging it back to the main CVXPY.
A tangential goal will be to finally replace CVXCORE; we noticed that the engine covers the affine extraction case CVXPY requires and have hopes of cleaning up that part of the codebase.
Although one big question would be about support for parameters and more complicated operations (broadcasting, N-dimensional expressions, etc).

My second goal is more broad and vague; it is about enjoying life and trying to not work so much.
I would like to do the following things this year:
- spend quality time with family and especially grandparents during my trip to China in may.
- visit some friends in Europe during the summer (Scotland, England, Switzerland and Germany).
- take a lot of rest during the remainder of the summer and do outdoor activities with some friends.
- give back to the great people in the LORER lab, in the form of discussions and helping out in the class as a (unofficial) TA.

I am looking forward to another great year of learning and meeting new people.
I am also eager to figure out what I will be doing next year (either PhD or join the industry).
I also hope I can graduate from this master's thesis and write a few more blogposts as well.

Here's to another great year and remember: \"it's for the love of the game!\".
